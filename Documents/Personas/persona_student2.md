### Bio

Franco is a curious and outgoing teenager who enjoys hands-on learning and solving real-world problems. He’s passionate about technology and often helps his family with setting up new devices. He’s particularly interested in computers, gaming, and the internet, but he’s also starting to become aware of the hidden risks that come with them—like data tracking and privacy issues.

At school, he sometimes finds traditional lectures uninspiring but becomes fully engaged when the content is presented through interactive activities or games. Franco values learning when it feels relevant and practical, especially if it connects with his interests in tech and gaming.


### Interests
* Online gaming (especially strategy or puzzle-based games)
* Building PCs and exploring software
* Watching YouTube tutorials about coding, ethical hacking, or tech news
* Smart home gadgets and automation
* Social media (but increasingly skeptical about privacy)


### Needs and expectations

* A classroom activity that feels more like a game than a lecture
* A game that explains real tech topics like data sovereignty in a way that doesn’t feel dumbed down
* The ability to explore and solve challenges with his classmates (team-based)
* Something that connects tech concepts to real-life tools and devices he’s already familiar with (like smart assistants, apps, etc.)
* Visuals and gameplay that are modern and not too “kiddy”


### Pain Points

* Getting bored by long, theory-heavy lessons
* Teachers who don't understand the tech as well as he does
* Feeling like school is behind the times when it comes to digital life
* Worries that adults don't take online privacy seriously enough



### How She Interacts with the Software

Franco dives into the SmartEscapeRoom game with enthusiasm. He treats it like a mission, exploring every part of the virtual smart home and trying to unlock all the puzzles. He enjoys playing in a group, often taking the lead and explaining tricky parts to classmates.
He appreciates that the game doesn’t lecture but lets him learn by doing—and the fact that it touches on real-world tech risks makes him feel like the topic matters. He’s also the type to finish the game and then Google more about how companies use his data online.
